## 1. Single-cell lineage tracing technologies and multimodal computational methods

We develop and optimize CRISPR-based lineage-recording systems, genetic barcodes, single-cell genomic assays, and computational phylogenetic methods. Our goal is to connect cell ancestry with molecular state in the same biological system. Spatial lineage tracing and multimodal integration are central to this program because tumor evolution unfolds within tissue architecture, not in isolation.

**Established foundation.** Previous work used base-editor-enabled DNA barcoding to reconstruct high-resolution phylogenies across 260,922 cells in mouse intestinal tumorigenesis and combined this evidence with human genomic data to support a polyclonal-to-monoclonal model of colorectal precancer evolution.

**Ongoing direction.** We are extending lineage recording toward spatially resolved, multimodal measurements and building computational workflows that jointly analyze lineage, transcriptomic, genomic, and microenvironmental information.

## 2. Clonal evolution and phenotypic plasticity in cancer metastasis

Metastasis requires more than dissemination. Tumor cells must survive bottlenecks, colonize distant organs, change state, and interact with a new microenvironment. We investigate clonal dissemination and metastatic colonization using lineage-resolved experimental models, longitudinal clinical cohorts, single-cell multi-omics, spatial transcriptomics, and genomic variation.

**Established foundation.** Dr. Lu's previous research has examined longitudinal tumor evolution, lineage plasticity, and phylogenetic reconstruction in cancer and developmental systems.

**Ongoing direction.** Current and proposed studies focus on how clone-specific fitness, phenotypic plasticity, and local ecological interactions shape spatial clonal dynamics and tumor-microenvironment co-evolution.

## 3. Adaptive evolution of radiation resistance and cancer recurrence

Radiation and drug treatment impose strong selective pressures. We study how therapy reshapes clone composition, cell fate, and tumor ecology, and how these changes contribute to residual disease, resistance, and recurrence. Experimental systems include genetically engineered mouse models, patient-derived xenografts, patient-derived organoids, and clinically annotated samples.

**Ongoing direction.** This program will map therapy-induced selection and cell-state remodeling, identify evolutionary routes to radiation and drug resistance, and test intervention strategies designed to prevent or constrain recurrence. These are active research aims, not reported clinical outcomes.
